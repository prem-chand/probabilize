Robots Under Uncertainty: Estimating Position from Noisy Sensors

A rover is in the aisle. It has to stop at a shelf, and the only position it can hold is a blend of a prediction and a noisy measurement.

Sensor trust 0.50. Sensor noise 0.50. Estimate 3.35 m.

The amber square is the prediction at 3.50 m, and it does not move. The teal dot is the measurement at 3.21 m, and the cloud around it widens as sensor noise rises. The ink line is the true position at 3.00 m. The rover never gets that number. The open circle is the estimate. It slides from the prediction toward the measurement as sensor trust rises.

NumberWhat it isCan the rover compute it?
True positionWhere the rover physically is, the ink lineNo
PredictionWhere the motion model says it is, the amber squareYes
MeasurementWhat the sensor reports, the teal dotYes, with noise
EstimateThe blend the open circle showsYes, and later decisions use this

The two sliders are one weight. Sensor trust is the share given to the measurement. Sensor noise is one minus that share, so dragging either slider moves the other. The estimate is the prediction plus that weight times the gap:

estimate=prediction+weight×(measurement−prediction)\text{estimate} = \text{prediction} + \text{weight} \times (\text{measurement} - \text{prediction})estimate=prediction+weight×(measurement−prediction)

Set sensor trust to 0.80. The circle sits at 3.27 m, because 3.50+0.80×(3.21−3.50)=3.273.50 + 0.80 \times (3.21 - 3.50) = 3.273.50+0.80×(3.21−3.50)=3.27. Trust 0 leaves the circle on the prediction. Trust 1 puts it on the measurement. Why 0.80 is the right share, and how a shove the motion model never saw shows up in a longer trace, is the next page.

Learning Outcomes
  • Separate true position, prediction, measurement, and estimate on the picture
  • Move one weight and predict which way the estimate circle goes
  • Compute the blend for a stated weight
Prerequisites

Robotics Foundations: Sensors, Actions, and Motion is the sense, decide, act loop, and why every sensor is imperfect. From the probability track, Probability Spaces and Events through Bayes' Rule and Generative Classification are the background for the claim that a reading is evidence about the state, not the state itself.

Browser lab: one blend#

The cell computes the same blend the circle shows. Change weight, press Run, and check the printed estimate against the picture.

python · runs in browser
prediction = 3.50
measurement = 3.21
weight = 0.80
estimate = prediction + weight * (measurement - prediction)
print(f"estimate {estimate:.2f} m")

Key takeaways#

  • The ink line is the truth. The rover does not have it.
  • The amber square is the prediction and the teal dot is the measurement.
  • One weight blends them. Noise is the complement of trust, so the sliders agree.
  • The open circle is the estimate, and it is what later decisions should use.

Further reading#

The derivation of that weight, the gain formula, and the longer tracking lab are in The Position Update.

  • Sebastian Thrun, Wolfram Burgard, and Dieter Fox, Probabilistic Robotics (MIT Press, 2005). Chapter 3 is the Kalman filter this picture is a one-number case of.
  • Greg Welch and Gary Bishop, An Introduction to the Kalman Filter (University of North Carolina, TR 95-041). The same gain, with the covariance algebra written out.
