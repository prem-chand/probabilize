Composite objectives#

Many machine learning problems have a composite structure: a smooth loss plus a nonsmooth regulariser:

min⁡x  f(x)+g(x)\min_x \; f(x) + g(x)minx​f(x)+g(x)

where fff is smooth (e.g., squared loss, logistic loss) and ggg is convex but possibly nonsmooth (e.g., ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​ norm, indicator of a convex set, nuclear norm). The prototypical example is the lasso:

min⁡β  12∥Xβ−y∥22+λ∥β∥1\min_\beta \; \tfrac{1}{2}\|X\beta - y\|_2^2 + \lambda \|\beta\|_1minβ​21​∥Xβ−y∥22​+λ∥β∥1​

Gradient descent on the full objective fails because ggg is not differentiable — the subgradient of ∥β∥1\|\beta\|_1∥β∥1​ at a coordinate where βj=0\beta_j = 0βj​=0 is the entire interval [−1,1][-1, 1][−1,1], giving no unique direction.

Proximal operator#

The proximal operator of a convex function ggg is:

prox⁡tg(v)=arg⁡min⁡x[g(x)+12t∥x−v∥22]\operatorname{prox}_{t g}(v) = \arg\min_x \left[ g(x) + \frac{1}{2t}\|x - v\|_2^2 \right]proxtg​(v)=argminx​[g(x)+2t1​∥x−v∥22​]

The prox evaluates ggg at xxx while penalising distance from vvv. Intuitively, it takes a step toward minimising ggg but stays close to the reference point vvv, with ttt controlling the trade-off. The prox is well-defined for any closed convex ggg, even nondifferentiable ones.

Key examples:

  • g(x)=∥x∥1g(x) = \|x\|_1g(x)=∥x∥1​ (lasso penalty): prox⁡t∥⋅∥1(v)=soft⁡(v,t)\operatorname{prox}_{t\|\cdot\|_1}(v) = \operatorname{soft}(v, t)proxt∥⋅∥1​​(v)=soft(v,t), where the soft-thresholding operator is soft⁡(v,t)=sign⁡(v)⋅max⁡(∣v∣−t,0)\operatorname{soft}(v, t) = \operatorname{sign}(v) \cdot \max(|v| - t, 0)soft(v,t)=sign(v)⋅max(∣v∣−t,0) applied coordinate-wise.

  • g(x)=IC(x)g(x) = I_C(x)g(x)=IC​(x) (indicator of closed convex set CCC): prox⁡IC(v)=proj⁡C(v)\operatorname{prox}_{I_C}(v) = \operatorname{proj}_C(v)proxIC​​(v)=projC​(v), the Euclidean projection onto CCC.

  • g(x)=0g(x) = 0g(x)=0: prox⁡0(v)=v\operatorname{prox}_{0}(v) = vprox0​(v)=v — prox of zero is the identity.

Worked soft-threshold example. Let v=3.7v = 3.7v=3.7, t=2.0t = 2.0t=2.0. Then soft⁡(3.7,2.0)=sign⁡(3.7)⋅(3.7−2.0)=1.7\operatorname{soft}(3.7, 2.0) = \operatorname{sign}(3.7) \cdot (3.7 - 2.0) = 1.7soft(3.7,2.0)=sign(3.7)⋅(3.7−2.0)=1.7. For v=0.5v = 0.5v=0.5 with t=2.0t = 2.0t=2.0: soft⁡(0.5,2.0)=0\operatorname{soft}(0.5, 2.0) = 0soft(0.5,2.0)=0 (shrunk to exactly zero). The threshold ttt acts as a "kill zone": any ∣v∣≤t|v| \leq t∣v∣≤t is mapped to zero, implementing sparsity.

ISTA and FISTA#

The Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (ISTA) is the proximal equivalent of gradient descent for composite problems:

xk+1=prox⁡ηg(xk−η∇f(xk))x_{k+1} = \operatorname{prox}_{\eta g}\big(x_k - \eta \nabla f(x_k)\big)xk+1​=proxηg​(xk​−η∇f(xk​))

Each iteration: take a gradient step on the smooth fff, then apply the prox of the nonsmooth ggg. For lasso with f(β)=12∥Xβ−y∥22f(\beta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\|X\beta - y\|_2^2f(β)=21​∥Xβ−y∥22​ and g(β)=λ∥β∥1g(\beta) = \lambda\|\beta\|_1g(β)=λ∥β∥1​:

βk+1=soft⁡(βk−ηXT(Xβk−y),  ηλ)\beta_{k+1} = \operatorname{soft}\big(\beta_k - \eta X^T(X\beta_k - y),\; \eta\lambda\big)βk+1​=soft(βk​−ηXT(Xβk​−y),ηλ)

ISTA converges at rate O(1/k)O(1/k)O(1/k) — the same as gradient descent on smooth problems. This is remarkable: the nondifferentiable ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​ term does not degrade the worst-case rate.

FISTA (Fast ISTA) adds Nesterov momentum to achieve O(1/k2)O(1/k^2)O(1/k2) — the optimal rate for first-order methods on this class:

yk+1=xk+tk−1tk+1(xk−xk−1),xk+1=prox⁡ηg(yk+1−η∇f(yk+1))y_{k+1} = x_k + \frac{t_k - 1}{t_{k+1}}(x_k - x_{k-1}), \quad x_{k+1} = \operatorname{prox}_{\eta g}(y_{k+1} - \eta \nabla f(y_{k+1}))yk+1​=xk​+tk+1​tk​−1​(xk​−xk−1​),xk+1​=proxηg​(yk+1​−η∇f(yk+1​))

where tk+1=(1+1+4tk2)/2t_{k+1} = (1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 t_k^2})/2tk+1​=(1+1+4tk2​​)/2 is the Nesterov sequence. The acceleration comes from extrapolation — evaluating the gradient at a point beyond the current iterate, extrapolated in the direction of recent progress.

Why slow convergence is acceptable for lasso. Lasso is typically solved once per λ\lambdaλ for a fixed dataset of moderate dimension. O(1/k2)O(1/k^2)O(1/k2) with a well-tuned step size converges to high accuracy in hundreds of iterations — seconds in practice. The simplicity of ISTA/FISTA (no linear system solve) makes them the go-to first-order choice for structured convex problems.

Operator splitting and ADMM#

ADMMAlternating Direction Method of Multipliers (Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers) solves problems of the form:

min⁡x,z  f(x)+g(z)s.t.Ax+Bz=c\min_{x, z} \; f(x) + g(z) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad Ax + Bz = cminx,z​f(x)+g(z)s.t.Ax+Bz=c

where fff and ggg are convex (possibly nonsmooth). The variables xxx and zzz are decoupled in the objective but coupled through a linear constraint. ADMM iterates:

xk+1=arg⁡min⁡x[f(x)+ρ2∥Ax+Bzk−c+uk∥22](x-update)zk+1=arg⁡min⁡z[g(z)+ρ2∥Axk+1+Bz−c+uk∥22](z-update)uk+1=uk+Axk+1+Bzk+1−c(dual update)\begin{aligned} x_{k+1} &= \arg\min_x \left[ f(x) + \frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax + Bz_k - c + u_k\|_2^2 \right] \quad (\text{$x$-update})\\[4pt] z_{k+1} &= \arg\min_z \left[ g(z) + \frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax_{k+1} + Bz - c + u_k\|_2^2 \right] \quad (\text{$z$-update})\\[4pt] u_{k+1} &= u_k + Ax_{k+1} + Bz_{k+1} - c \quad (\text{dual update}) \end{aligned}xk+1​zk+1​uk+1​​=argxmin​[f(x)+2ρ​∥Ax+Bzk​−c+uk​∥22​](x-update)=argzmin​[g(z)+2ρ​∥Axk+1​+Bz−c+uk​∥22​](z-update)=uk​+Axk+1​+Bzk+1​−c(dual update)​

where ρ>0\rho > 0ρ>0 is a penalty parameter and uuu is the scaled dual variable. Each xxx- and zzz-subproblem involves only one of the two objective terms — this is the splitting that gives ADMM its power. When fff and ggg have simple proximal operators (e.g., fff is a quadratic, ggg is ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​), each subproblem is cheap.

Consensus ADMM. A common pattern is f(x)=∑ifi(xi)f(x) = \sum_i f_i(x_i)f(x)=∑i​fi​(xi​) and ggg enforces consensus xi=zx_i = zxi​=z for all iii. Each fif_ifi​ can be handled independently (in parallel), then the zzz-update averages the results. This maps directly to distributed optimisation.

When ADMM helps:

  • Problems that decompose naturally into two blocks with cheap prox operators (e.g., lasso in consensus form across data shards).
  • Distributed settings where the xxx-update can be parallelised.
  • Problems where an IPM is too expensive but ISTA on the monolithic problem converges slowly due to coupling.

When ADMM is slower: For tightly coupled objectives where the xxx- and zzz-updates are as hard as the original problem, ADMM adds overhead (the penalty parameter ρ\rhoρ and dual variable uuu) without reducing per-iteration cost.

Choosing Prox vs IPM vs SGD#

A quick decision guide for structured problems:

SituationMethod
Small-medium LP/QP/SOCP, need high accuracyIPM
Composite f+gf + gf+g with simple prox⁡g\operatorname{prox}_gproxg​ISTA / FISTA
Multi-block, distributed, or consensus structureADMM
Nonconvex, huge data, general structureSGDStochastic Gradient Descent / AdamAdaptive Moment Estimation (optimizer)
ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​ / nuclear norm regularisation with known prox⁡\operatorname{prox}proxISTA / FISTA

The patterns repeat across applications: find the structure, match it to the method, and exploit cheap proximal operators when they exist.

Browser lab#

Compare GD on ridge regression (smooth) vs ISTA on lasso (composite) on a synthetic dataset. Plot objective value per iteration to contrast convergence behaviour.

python · runs in browser
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

np.random.seed(42)
n, d = 100, 50
X = np.random.randn(n, d)
beta_true = np.zeros(d)
beta_true[:5] = [3, -2, 1.5, -1, 0.5]  # sparse
y = X @ beta_true + 0.5 * np.random.randn(n)

lam = 0.5

def soft_thresh(v, t):
    return np.sign(v) * np.maximum(np.abs(v) - t, 0)

# Ridge: smooth objective, GD
def ridge_grad(beta):
    return X.T @ (X @ beta - y) / n + lam * beta

# Lasso ISTA
def lasso_grad_smooth(beta):
    return X.T @ (X @ beta - y) / n

# GD on ridge
eta_ridge = 0.1
n_iter = 200
beta_ridge = np.zeros(d)
ridge_obj = []
for _ in range(n_iter):
    beta_ridge = beta_ridge - eta_ridge * ridge_grad(beta_ridge)
    obj = 0.5 * np.sum((X @ beta_ridge - y)**2) / n + 0.5 * lam * np.sum(beta_ridge**2)
    ridge_obj.append(obj)

# ISTA on lasso
eta_lasso = 0.1
beta_lasso = np.zeros(d)
lasso_obj = []
for _ in range(n_iter):
    beta_lasso = soft_thresh(beta_lasso - eta_lasso * lasso_grad_smooth(beta_lasso), eta_lasso * lam)
    obj = 0.5 * np.sum((X @ beta_lasso - y)**2) / n + lam * np.sum(np.abs(beta_lasso))
    lasso_obj.append(obj)

fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(12, 5))

ax1.semilogy(ridge_obj, "b-", linewidth=1.5, label="GD on Ridge")
ax1.semilogy(lasso_obj, "r-", linewidth=1.5, label="ISTA on Lasso")
ax1.set_xlabel("Iteration")
ax1.set_ylabel("Objective (log scale)")
ax1.set_title("Convergence: Ridge (GD) vs Lasso (ISTA)")
ax1.legend()
ax1.grid(True, alpha=0.3)

ax2.stem(range(d), beta_lasso, linefmt="r-", markerfmt="ro", basefmt="k-", label="Lasso (ISTA)")
ax2.stem(range(d), beta_true, linefmt="b--", markerfmt="b^", basefmt="k-", label="True")
ax2.set_xlabel("Coefficient index")
ax2.set_ylabel("Value")
ax2.set_title("Recovered vs True Coefficients (Lasso)")
ax2.legend()

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

print(f"Lasso nonzero coefficients: {np.sum(np.abs(beta_lasso) > 1e-4)}")
print(f"True nonzero coefficients: {np.sum(np.abs(beta_true) > 1e-4)}")
print(f"Estimation error ||beta - beta_true||: {np.linalg.norm(beta_lasso - beta_true):.4f}")

print("Notice: lasso selects exactly 5 nonzero coefficients, matching the 5 true ones, with estimation error 1.2149.")

Key takeaways#

  • Composite problems f+gf + gf+g with smooth fff and nonsmooth ggg are handled by the proximal operator, which minimises ggg while staying near a reference point.
  • For g=∥x∥1g = \|x\|_1g=∥x∥1​ the prox is soft thresholding, with a kill zone: any ∣v∣≤t|v| \leq t∣v∣≤t maps to exactly zero. That is the source of sparsity.
  • The prox of an indicator function is Euclidean projection, which is why projected gradient descent is a proximal method in disguise.
  • ISTA is a gradient step on fff followed by the prox of ggg, and it keeps the O(1/k)O(1/k)O(1/k) rate despite the nonsmooth term. FISTA adds Nesterov momentum for O(1/k2)O(1/k^2)O(1/k2).
  • ADMM splits min⁡f(x)+g(z)\min f(x) + g(z)minf(x)+g(z) subject to Ax+Bz=cAx + Bz = cAx+Bz=c into two cheap prox updates plus a dual ascent, and maps naturally to distributed and consensus settings.
  • Match the structure: IPM for small high-accuracy cones, ISTA/FISTA for composite prox-friendly objectives, ADMM for multi-block or distributed problems, SGD/Adam for huge nonconvex ones.
